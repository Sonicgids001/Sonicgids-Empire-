Why your engagement rate is lying to you

Analytics21 Jul 20266 min read

Engagement rate is easy to inflate and easy to misread. It tells you whether content resonates — not whether it sells. Here is the dashboard we actually use, and the six numbers we report to clients instead.

The six numbers that matter

  • Reach and reach source: how much is organic versus paid, and from which placements.
  • Saves and sends: the strongest signal of genuine value.
  • Profile visits and follows: whether strangers want more of you.
  • Conversations started: DMs, WhatsApp clicks, form submissions.
  • Cost per result and ROAS: what you actually paid for each outcome.
  • Retention: how many customers come back.

Build one source of truth

Platform numbers, GA4 or Firebase events, and a spreadsheet of real sales should reconcile. When they don't, trust the sales data and fix your tracking. We configure events for leads, phone taps, WhatsApp clicks and purchases before reporting anything.

Report in plain language

Every report should answer three questions: what happened, why we think it happened, and what we're doing next. If a metric can't change a decision, leave it out.

Watch leading indicators weekly

Sales lag. Saves, sends, profile visits and DMs lead. Track those weekly so you can adjust creative before the revenue number arrives — instead of explaining it afterwards.

Key takeaways

  • Engagement rate alone cannot tell you whether you're growing a business.
  • Track saves, sends, conversations, cost per result and retention.
  • Reconcile platform data with real sales records.
  • Every report should end with a decision, not a chart.
  • Use leading indicators weekly to stay ahead of revenue.
